Two-Lane Merge (Stage 3)

A two-lane main road from x = 0 to 4,000 m (folded into four 1,000 m rows) with a 500 m one-lane on-ramp that joins the right lane at x = 2,500 m. Cars leave at point C, x = 4,000 m. The ramp meter sits at the ramp entry, and released cars start from a stop. Each car's desired speed is drawn from a Gaussian curve. Cars follow the Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) and change lanes when it pays off and is safe (a MOBIL-style rule). Ramp cars use a zipper merge from the acceleration lane. The ramp holds at most nmax cars, and arrivals beyond that are turned away (spillback). Detectors A–D record 30-second counts per lane.

Layout (default geometry)

The whole 4,000 m section drawn to scale. Yellow circles are detectors: A on the main road at 1,500 m, B at the ramp entry, C on the ramp queue at the meter, and D on the main road at 3,300 m. The sliders below can move the merge and ramp; this diagram shows the defaults.

Breakdown and wave speed

A breakdown starts when the right-lane space-mean speed at detector A stays below 60% of the mean desired speed for three intervals (1.5 min), and ends after two intervals back above it. The first 10 minutes are skipped while the road fills. The breakdown flow is the total flow at D over the 5 min before onset. The queue discharge flow is the flow at D during the breakdown. Wave speed is measured three ways: cross-correlating congested speeds 800 ft apart in the x-t speed field (last 10 min, upstream-moving waves only), from the shockwave equation ω = (qfree − qcong) / (kfree − kcong) using detector A's states before and after onset, and from Newell's theory, −(l + s0) / T.

Flow-density (q-k) diagram

Each dot is one 30 s interval at detector A or D, per lane. The yellow curve is the IDM equilibrium q-k relationship using the current mean desired speed, time gap, s0 and car length. Its peak is the model's theoretical lane capacity.
